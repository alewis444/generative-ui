ATOMIC — COMPOSING FROM ATOMS
How it works

This is the harder version: instead of choosing one of five finished screens, the model composes a response from six real, fixed components, deciding which ones, how many, and what data goes in each, for this specific task. What it can't do that Shape also can't: invent a component that isn't in the catalog below.

ADJUST — NEGOTIATED COMPOSITION
How it works

Atomic is one-shot: task in, composition out, done. Here you can talk back, and the model edits the existing composition instead of starting over. You can swap a component's type ("swap the checklist for a stat bar"), add one ("add a card for the caterer," within the 3–6 limit), remove one, or edit a component's data in place ("give the budget a $50 buffer"). Added or changed components get marked; anything unmentioned stays as it was.

COMMON USE CASE — EXPLORE VS. DIRECT PATH

Two entry points, because they're two different jobs.

One box trying to serve both "I know exactly what I want" and "help me figure out what I want" tends to serve neither well. This splits them at the door, and only brings them back together once something from exploring has actually converged.

Not a fourth difficulty tier. Shape, Atomic, and Adjust are a real ladder where each one is strictly harder to build and contains the last. This isn't harder than any of them; it's a decision about which entry pattern a person meets first, built on top of the mechanisms already there. The precision option below just calls the Atomic mechanic directly; the explore option's handoff drops straight into Adjust's negotiation state.

IRRESPONSIBLE — NO CLOSED SET, NO RULES

What happens when nothing is fixed.

Shape picks from 5 screens. Atomic composes from 6 components. Adjust edits under standing rules that always win. This is what's left once you remove all three — the model free-styling a UI for the same brief, with no shared vocabulary and no memory of the last answer.
